Help desk

How CuStorA works, in plain words. Open any section.

Start here: the whole idea in 30 seconds

CuStorA looks at how your customers have bought in the past, estimates who is likely to stop buying, works out how much money that would cost you, and tells your team who to contact first.

  1. Bring in your data on Upload & data health: a transaction history, a customer table, or the one-click real retail demo.
  2. Run the analysis. A 12-stage pipeline runs in the background and you can watch it. When it finishes, stay on that page and click any stage to see what happened to your data.
  3. Open the Retention command center to see how much revenue is at risk and the action queue of customers to contact.
  4. Explore under Analyse: the risk × value matrix, the outreach planner and the customer clusters.
  5. Contact people, then mark them as reached. They move to Reached customers so nobody is chased twice.
  6. Check the proof on Model evidence (at the bottom of this menu) whenever you want to know how far to trust the numbers.
What each page is for
Retention command center
The main page. Four key figures, where the loss is concentrated by segment, what the model looks at, and the action queue: every customer still to contact. Search, filter by state, sort, and click a customer to open a side panel with the reasons and the suggested action.
Reached customers
Customers your team has already contacted. They are removed from the queue, the matrix and the planner. You can move anyone back.
Risk × value matrix
Every customer as a dot: further right = more likely to leave, higher up = more revenue at stake. Pick one of the four groups to see what to do with it.
Outreach planner
Set how many customers your team can contact, how many of them you could win back, and what each contact costs. It shows what that buys, and exports the call list.
Customer clusters
A map of your customers grouped by similar buying behaviour, with a short profile of each group.
Upload & data health
Bring in data, check it, and start an analysis.
Model evidence
How the model was tested and how accurate it really is, including its limits.
Words used in the app
Churn / chance of leaving
The model's estimate of how likely a customer is to stop buying. With a transaction file, "leaves" means no purchase in the next N days (N is the window shown on the dashboard).
Revenue at stake
What a customer normally spends in that window.
Expected loss
Chance of leaving × revenue at stake. It is the number used to rank who to contact first.
Win-back rate (planner)
The share of contacted leavers you think you can keep. It is your assumption, not a prediction. Use your own past results if you have them.
Segment
A group of customers who buy in a similar way, found automatically and given a readable name such as "High-Value, Frequent Customers".
RFM
Recency (how recently they bought), Frequency (how often) and Monetary (how much). Each customer gets a 1 to 5 score on each.
Relationship health
A 0 to 100 score for how healthy the relationship looks, separate from the churn chance. It is Healthy, At Risk or Critical.
ROC-AUC
How well the model ranks leavers above stayers on customers it never saw. 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect. Around 0.75 is realistic for churn.

The business priority adds how valuable the customer is. Critical is a high-value customer with a high chance of leaving, or anyone in the top 1% of expected loss. High covers valuable customers at medium risk, medium-value customers at high risk, and customers in a Critical state. Medium and Normal are the rest.

The 12 stages of the analysis
  1. Cleaning: removes duplicates, fixes broken rows and formats.
  2. Feature Engineering: turns raw purchases into signals such as days since last purchase.
  3. RFM Analysis: scores each customer on recency, frequency and spend.
  4. PCA: squeezes the signals into a 2-D map for the clusters picture.
  5. K-Means Clustering: finds groups of customers who behave alike.
  6. Segment Naming: gives each group a readable name.
  7. Churn Prediction: trains (or reuses) a model and scores every customer's chance of leaving.
  8. Relationship Health: scores how healthy each relationship looks.
  9. SHAP Explainability: finds which behaviours push predictions up or down.
  10. Customer State: combines chance of leaving and health into one state.
  11. Retention Recommendations: suggests one action per customer.
  12. CLV and Business Metrics: works out money at stake and ranks who needs attention first.

After a run finishes, click stay here on the progress page, then click any stage to see what it did to your data.

Marking customers as reached

When you have contacted a customer, use Mark as reached in the customer side panel, on their profile, or on their row in the action queue.

  • They disappear from the action queue, the matrix and the planner, and the call-list export, so they are not chased again.
  • They appear on Reached customers with the date and what their risk and suggested action were at that moment.
  • Marked by mistake? Use Move back to queue on the Reached page.
  • The reached list belongs to the dataset you analysed. If you analyse a different dataset, the old list is not applied to it.
Tips for reading the screens
  • Short text with a small ⓘ shows more when you hover over it (or tap it on a touch screen). Move away and it shrinks back.
  • The sun/moon button at the top right switches light and dark mode.
  • On the Risk × value matrix, hover a dot for its numbers and click it to open the customer.
  • Under the sidebar's Jump to a state list, click a state to filter the action queue.
Good to know (honest limits)
  • Every figure is an estimate from past behaviour. Use it to decide who to contact first, not as a certainty about one person.
  • Synthetic demo data is made up. It only shows that the software runs, and its accuracy numbers are not evidence. A banner on the dashboard always says which kind of data you are looking at.
  • The clusters map is a 2-D simplification, so groups can overlap on it even when they differ clearly in the numbers.
  • The full test results and limitations are on Model evidence.
Common questions
A customer I expected is missing from the queue.
They have probably been marked as reached. Look on Reached customers and move them back if needed.
Where did the matrix and the planner go?
Each has its own page under Analyse in the sidebar.
Why do I not see "How far to trust the ranking" numbers?
Those come from testing on a later period, which needs a transaction file. With a customer table or the synthetic demo, that test is not available.
The analysis page says it is running, how long will it take?
Usually under a minute for the demo. Larger files take longer. The page updates itself, so there is no need to refresh.
