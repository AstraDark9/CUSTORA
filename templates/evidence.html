Limitations (stated plainly)

  • ROC-AUC around 0.75 is a realistic result for churn on one year of retail data: the model ranks customers usefully but cannot predict individuals with certainty. Use it to prioritise outreach, not as a verdict. A much higher score on data like this would be a red flag for leakage.
  • The share of customers who leave moves with the season (about 43% in spring, 31% in the autumn test period). Probabilities are calibrated to the history seen, so expect them to run a little high or low when the season turns; the ranking is what stays reliable.
  • “Churn” here means no purchase in the chosen window. In a business where customers legitimately buy once a year, choose a longer window.
  • Only customers who bought recently are scored. Customers already lapsed are a win-back problem, not a prediction problem.
  • Relationship health weights and the customer-state rules are documented judgement calls (see the README), not values fitted from outcomes.
  • Results come from one retailer's history. Accuracy on a different business should be re-measured on that business's own data — which is exactly what uploading a transaction file does.
  • Synthetic demo data exists only to exercise the software. It is labelled wherever it appears and its accuracy figures are not evidence.
